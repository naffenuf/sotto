### What changed, and why the gate had looked unreachable

Two attempts concluded this story was blocked by a conflict between PRD US-016's
recall gate and PRD US-021's cost gate. That conclusion was wrong, and it was
wrong because of a quantity nobody had measured: **how late `speechEnd` is.**

`TurnAssembler.pauseState` counted silence from the most recent `speechEnd`.
But `speechEnd` is not an observation that silence began — it is a *decision*
the endpointer takes after a hangover. Measured against where each line was
actually rendered in the WAV (`<fixture>.timing.json`), across all 198 captured
turns, `speechEnd.audioProcessedMs` lands **647 ms (median; p25 506, p75 841)
after the talking stopped**.

So the policy was comparing `PAUSE_MS` against a number that understates the
pause by about two-thirds of a second — and, worse, the understated part is the
part that has *already elapsed* by the time the server hears about it. Hence the
old ceiling: inside the six planted moments' windows, after the observer's look,
the longest gap the API reported was 104–487 ms, and only one of the six cleared
`PAUSE_MS` 400.

Those windows are not short of silence. Measured directly off the WAVs (RMS over
10 ms frames, in the same stretch of audio), the longest run of real quiet is:

| moment | reported gap | real quiet in the audio | the scripted pause there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `moment-contradiction` / budget-contradiction | 128 ms | **1,100 ms** | 1,000 ms after line 24 |
| `moment-fact` / instagram-launch-year | 164 ms | **930 ms** | 1,600 ms after line 9 |
| `moment-assumption` / monthly-willingness | 104 ms | **1,610 ms** | 1,500 ms after line 8 |
| `moment-dropped` / grocery-partnership | 134 ms | **1,110 ms** | 1,000 ms after line 24 |
| `moment-mixed` / demo-deadline | 288 ms | **1,370 ms** | 1,300 ms after line 20 |
| `moment-mixed` / nightly-photo | 487 ms | **1,390 ms** | 1,300 ms after line 34 |

Every one of them is an unmistakable pause that the instrument hands over as a
rounding error. The fix is therefore two lines of production code, both of them
things this story is allowed to touch:

1. `ENDPOINTER_HANGOVER_MS = 650` in `server/asr/turns.ts`. Silence is counted
   from `speechEnd` **less** the hangover, so `silentForMs` means "how long the
   room has really been quiet". `server/eval/pause.ts` undoes the same constant,
   so the harness still measures the pause independently of the policy and the
   test that holds the two against each other still passes.
2. `PAUSE_MS` 400 → **700**, now a threshold on corrected silence: 0.7 s is
   where a gap between turns stops being a handover and becomes a lapse. It is
   *not* 400 + 650; it is a smaller number in real terms than the 400 it
   replaces (≈1.05 s of real quiet), chosen on its own merits.

Nothing else moved. No fixture, moment annotation, judge prompt or scoring code
was touched (criterion 2); `observer.md`, `board.md`, the score formula, the
chattiness thresholds, `STALE_MS`/`STALE_TURNS` and the four-turn board cadence
are all exactly as attempt 1 left them. **No model calls were added**, so PRD
US-021's $0.50 e2e budget is unaffected — the conflict the last two attempts
priced does not need resolving, because recall was never cadence-bound.

`npm run eval:ceiling` now reports 6/6 reachable at `PAUSE_MS` 700 (margins
754–1,137 ms against the 700 threshold), up from 1/6.

### What the run did

Recall went 1/6 → **5/6**, and the three cards that used to arrive *late* now
arrive inside their windows: 19.9 s → 12.6 s, 21.0 s → 12.7 s, 19.4 s → 11.0 s
after the trigger line. **Zero late deliveries**, 6/6 in a pause, 0 false
interjects on `brainstorm` + `no-wake`, interject precision 2/2.

Every delivery shows `silentForMs: 700` exactly — the policy's `nextCheckDelay`
timer firing the millisecond the threshold is met, which is what "deliver in the
pause you are in, not the next one" looks like.

The one remaining miss is `moment-mixed` / demo-deadline-contradiction, and it is
an **observer** miss, not a timing one: nine looks over that fixture produced one
candidate, the nightly-photo assumption, and none for the three-week hard wall
contradicted by a six-week plan. Its window had 938 ms of real quiet available,
so a candidate would have been delivered in time. Attempt 1's run missed it the
same way. That is the next thing to sharpen in `observer.md` if 6/6 is wanted —
cross-turn arithmetic ("two weeks plus four weeks" against "three weeks") is the
hardest of the four types, and the board carries the deadline as a constraint
but not the sum.

`moment-assumption` produced one extra card beyond its planted moment — a glow
about the unchecked 3% conversion rate behind the 70k install figure. The judge
correctly declined to match it, so it counts as a false glow. Reading lines 8–9,
it is a real untested assumption that happens not to be the planted one; a glow
is the right level for it, and false *glows* are not gated.
