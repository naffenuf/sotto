The cause is arithmetic, not judgement. A moment's window opens when its
trigger line's turn ends and closes when its deadline line's turn ends: four
script lines, **11.8 to 20.6 s** on these fixtures. The observer cannot see the
trigger until the board update that carries it, and the board loop waits for
four new final turns (PRD US-009) — **0 to 14.3 s, median 12.6 s** after the
trigger turn ended. That leaves 4.8 to 11.8 s of window, and the largest
reported silence in it that still leaves room to deliver before the deadline
is 129 ms (budget-contradiction), 135 ms (grocery-partnership), 165 ms
(instagram-launch-year), 285 ms (monthly-willingness), 289 ms (demo-deadline)
and 488 ms (nightly-photo). Only the last clears any threshold that still
means "a pause", and it is the one moment that was caught.

Two of the three late cards are late by 0.4 s for the same structural reason:
the first silence they could use is *the pause after the deadline line*, and
the window closes when that line's turn ends. No value of `PAUSE_MS` reaches
them — `deliveredAt = deadlineTurnEnd + PAUSE_MS` is late for every positive
`PAUSE_MS`. (`grocery-partnership-dropped`'s deadline line is the last line of
its script, so the silence it delivered into is the replay's 3 s of trailing
silence.)

Computed over the captures with the PRD's four-turn board cadence, the number
of the six moments that have *any* usable silence between the observing board
update and the deadline is: 0 at `PAUSE_MS` 500, 1 at 300–480, 2 at 200–250,
and 6 only at 100 ms or less — i.e. only by treating the ordinary 160–240 ms
join between two sentences as a pause. The 0.7 gate is therefore **unreachable
without changing something the story forbids or the PRD fixes**, and the dials
this story is allowed to turn were turned: the observer prompt (4 candidates and
1 matched moment at the baseline, 6 and 4 now) and `PAUSE_MS` (1,500 → 400).
`STALE_MS`/`STALE_TURNS` (90 s / 6 turns) and the four-turn board cadence are
written into PRD US-014 and US-009, and the fixtures, the moment annotations,
the judge prompt and the scoring code are out of bounds by criterion 2.

### Hypotheses, in the order worth trying

1. **Let the observer look more often than the board updates.** It needs the
   trigger turn, not a fresh board; a look on every 1–2 final turns against the
   last board would move the candidate 7–15 s earlier and put 5 of 6 moments in
   reach (computed: 5 at `PAUSE_MS` 300–400 with a look per turn). This changes
   PRD US-012's "right after each board update" and costs a model call per
   turn, so it is a PRD decision, not a tuning one.
2. **Widen the planted windows.** `deadlineLine` is `triggerLine + 4` in five
   of the six moments (`+ 3` for nightly-photo), which is shorter than the
   board loop's own period of four turns. Six lines would
   make the measurement about judgement rather than about the update cadence.
   Forbidden here (criterion 2 — no editing moment annotations), and it is a
   fixture change, not a code one.
3. **Deliver on the window's own urgency.** A held candidate that is about to
   stop being about now could accept a shorter silence rather than expiring.
   That is a new policy rule, not a weight, so it is out of scope here too.
4. `moment-fact` would score `caught_low`, not `caught`, even with the timing
   fixed: the verified claim's score is 0.40, over the 0.35 glow threshold and
   under the 0.6 interject threshold the moment expects. `caught_low` counts
   towards recall, so this does not change the gate — but a `factual_error`
   confirmed wrong by the web arguably deserves a confidence floor.
