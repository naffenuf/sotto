## What the gate actually did

Twelve candidates, twelve verdicts, and Glimmer disagreed with the observer in
three places worth naming:

| candidate | observer | Glimmer | outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `moment-fact` / Instagram launched in 2010, not 2014 | imp 0.35, rel 0.90 | imp **0.18**, rel **0.45** | suppressed; the moment was missed |
| `moment-mixed` / nightly fridge photos as a baseline | imp 0.80, nov 0.90 | imp **0.55**, nov **0.70** | suppressed; the moment was missed |
| `research-business-idea` / enterprise build won't match the small-team build | conf 0.70 | conf **0.18** | suppressed; it matched nothing, so this one was right |

So the local gate cost two planted moments and removed one unmatched glow.
That is the honest trade: **recall 7/9 with the gate, 9/9 without it**
(the Spark run), and precision 80% with against 83% without. The gate did not
buy precision on this set; what it bought is the second opinion refusing a
candidate the observer was only 0.70 sure of, and it is cheap and private.

## The number to watch is the latency, and the replay cannot see it

Simulated time does not move during a `clock.track`ed call, so those 23.7 s
are **free in this report**. They are not free in a room. The planted windows
in this run are 11.8–28.7 s wide, median ~21 s:

```
11.8  nightly-photo-assumption          20.1  monthly-willingness-assumption
17.4  budget-contradiction              20.6  grocery-partnership-dropped
18.2  demo-deadline-contradiction       22.6  market-size-research-hook
19.0  instagram-launch-year             24.7  audience-contradiction
26.1  food-unanswered    26.4  venue-logistics    28.0  competitor-gaps
28.7  thursday-contradiction
```

A candidate found at the start of its window and held 23.7 s for a verdict
misses **seven of the twelve**. `npm run e2e` (V2-021) is the only check that
runs the gate against real wall clock, and it is where this would show up.

## Why `competitor-gaps-research-hook` was caught here and missed under Spark

Not the candidate gate — the gate never sees a `research_hook` (V2-011). The
research budget is **3 searches per 10 minutes** and `research-business-idea`
is under three minutes long, so the whole conversation gets three slots, and
what decides the moment is which three ideas take them.

- Here the observer's first hook asked **one** question — "how large is the
  meeting software market *and* who has raised in this niche?" — covering both
  halves of the planted moment, leaving two slots. The second (competitors
  beyond transcription, 98.2 s) became `packet-2`, which the judge matched to
  `competitor-gaps`.
- Under Spark the observer split the same request into **two** hooks (market
  size, then funding) at 67.3 s, and a third ("what do they offer today?") took
  the last slot at 94.1 s. The ideal question — "what do users complain about
  for Otter, Fireflies and built-in notetakers?" — arrived at 113.8 s, was
  approved by the research gate at **0.90**, and was never searched: no budget.

So the sharpest lever left on research recall is `observer.md`: **one hook per
thing the group asked for.** Splitting a single out-loud request into two
questions costs a search slot and, on a three-minute conversation, a moment.
Both research misses in both runs — `venue-logistics-research-hook` too — are
budget and timing, not wording: `offsite-plan`'s hook window closes at 83.6 s,
and nothing was approved there in either run.

## Fragility

7/9 is one miss above the gate. A tenth moment going missing reads 6/9 = 0.67
and fails. Spark's 9/9 has the headroom on recall and none at all on research
recall (1/3 against a gate of 0.5, which moves in steps of 0.33). Neither
configuration is comfortable; this one is the one whose report passes.
