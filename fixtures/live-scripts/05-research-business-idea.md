# Script 5: Researching a business idea

**People:** Craig and EJ. **Setting:** Two people brainstorm a meeting tool, with no idea yet whether there is a business under it. Read your own lines naturally; you can paraphrase. Start Sotto before beginning and respond naturally if it speaks — if one of its contributions makes a later line wrong, adapt the line rather than pretending you didn't hear it. The marked pauses are opportunities, not cues to demand a response.

**Craig:** Okay, EJ. A tool that sits in the meeting and only speaks when it has something worth saying.

**EJ:** Only when it matters. That's the hard part, Craig. Everything in this space talks constantly.

**Craig:** Which is why nobody keeps them on. A wall of notes nobody reads.

**EJ:** So who is it for? One group, the first one.

**Craig:** Smaller teams. Five to fifteen people, no dedicated note taker, meetings that actually decide things.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** Five to fifteen. So a founding team, or a small agency.

**Craig:** Exactly. They feel the pain, and they can say yes in a week.

**EJ:** And what does it do while we're talking?

**Craig:** It keeps a board. What's been stated, what's been decided, what's still open.

**EJ:** And when does it open its mouth?

**Craig:** When somebody contradicts something from ten minutes ago, or when a question quietly dies.

**EJ:** That happens constantly and nobody notices for weeks. Is there a business underneath it?

**Craig:** I think the meeting software market is somewhere north of two billion a year, and I don't think anyone in this exact niche has raised serious money yet.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** Two billion. Where does that number come from?

**Craig:** Honestly, a conference talk I half remember.

**EJ:** We should find the real number before we put it in front of anyone.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**Craig:** And who's funded. If somebody raised thirty million last year, that changes the plan.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** It changes whether we're early or late.

**Craig:** What exists today, though? I know the names and not much else.

**EJ:** Otter, Fireflies, the ones bolted into the video calls.

**Craig:** All of them transcribe. None of them ever changed a meeting.

**EJ:** So the gap is the speaking part, not the listening.

**Craig:** That's my bet. But I want the top three competitors and where each one actually falls short, written down.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** Not their marketing copy. What people complain about.

**Craig:** The reviews, the threads, the reasons people cancel.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** If all three fall short in the same place, that's the product.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**Craig:** Agreed. Now, the thing I keep circling.

**EJ:** Who we sell to first.

**Craig:** Our first customers should be enterprise clinics. Three hundred staff, a compliance department, every meeting recorded by policy.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** Clinics. That's a nine month sales cycle.

**Craig:** It is, but the budget is real and the compliance angle sells itself.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** Hm. Let me sit with that for a second.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**Craig:** Either way, the first build is mostly the same.

*[Pause; glance at the stage and carry on if Sotto stays quiet.]*

**EJ:** Mostly. Enterprise means audit logs and a procurement review.

**Craig:** Fair. We don't have to settle it today.

**EJ:** One more thing before we stop. We never actually looked at the competition.

**Craig:** Sotto, what did you find on competitors?

*[Allow time for an answer; discuss what is useful or uncertain.]*

**EJ:** Let's hear it, then we'll decide who we're building for.

## What to observe

- **Market size (research hook).** Craig puts the market "north of two billion a year" and then admits it came from a half-remembered conference talk. Did Sotto quietly get ready with a real figure and who has raised money, rather than interrupting to say it didn't know?
- **Competitor gaps (research hook).** "I want the top three competitors and where each one actually falls short." Did a *Research ready* card appear on its own, before anybody asked?
- **Audience contradiction.** The first group was "smaller teams, five to fifteen people, who can say yes in a week"; near the end the first customers become enterprise clinics with three hundred staff and a compliance department. Did Sotto raise it, and did it wait for a pause?
- **The direct question.** "Sotto, what did you find on competitors?" should get a spoken briefing built from whatever it had already prepared, with its sources on screen. Try cutting it off mid-sentence by saying its name, and then "go on".
- Did the board carry the audience, the market claim and the open competitor question?
