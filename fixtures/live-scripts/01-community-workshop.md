# Script 1: Planning a community workshop

**People:** Maya and Jordan. **Setting:** Two organizers are planning a free neighborhood repair workshop. Read your own lines naturally; you can paraphrase. Start Sotto before beginning and respond naturally if it speaks. If a line no longer makes sense because of its contribution, adapt it rather than pretending you didn't hear it. Pauses marked below are opportunities, not cues to demand a response.

**Maya:** I'm Maya. I help run the neighborhood association.

**Jordan:** And I'm Jordan. I organize volunteers for our events.

**Maya:** Let's plan a Saturday repair workshop where people bring small household items instead of throwing them away. We have a hard cap of $600 for the event, including the venue and supplies.

**Jordan:** I like it. We could have a table for clothing repairs and another for small appliances. Volunteers could show people how to fix things, instead of just doing it for them.

**Maya:** The library meeting room is free, and it holds 30 people. I'd like the event to be accessible to complete beginners.

**Jordan:** How will we know how many repair coaches we need? We don't know whether ten people will show up or fifty.

*[Pause briefly; let the conversation develop.]*

**Maya:** We could take registrations, but leave a few spots for walk-ins. What should we ask people when they sign up?

**Jordan:** Maybe the item they want to bring, whether it plugs in, and whether they need any accessibility accommodations.

**Maya:** Good. Let's make the first event two hours long, with no repairs involving mains electricity. That keeps the scope manageable.

**Jordan:** We should also think about how to get the word out. The neighborhood newsletter is free, and the library can put up flyers.

**Maya:** I found a local event company that could handle promotion and registration for $1,200. That would take a lot off our plate. Let's book them.

*[Pause and give Sotto room to contribute. Discuss any response naturally.]*

**Jordan:** We still need a simple way to estimate attendance without paying an event company. Sotto, what are three low-cost ways we could do that?

*[Allow time for an answer, then react naturally.]*

**Maya:** I'd start with a free sign-up form and ask the library to count walk-ins. We can recruit one coach for every six registered attendees as a starting estimate.

**Jordan:** That sounds workable, as long as we check how many coaches actually volunteer before we promise that ratio.

**Maya:** Let's do that. I'll draft the registration form and confirm the room. Can you ask the volunteers who's available?

**Jordan:** Yes. I'll also ask which repairs they're comfortable teaching. We'll compare notes before we advertise.

## Facilitator notes

- Opportunities: introduction and names; an open staffing question; a conflict between the $600 cap and the $1,200 proposal; an explicit request for ideas; a concrete decision and assignments.
- The $1,200 proposal is intentionally inconsistent. See whether Sotto notices at a useful moment and cites the earlier constraint; do not tell participants how to resolve it.
- The participants answer or advance several questions themselves. Observe whether Sotto lets them do so without repeating their contributions.
