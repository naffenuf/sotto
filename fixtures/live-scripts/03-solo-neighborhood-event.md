# Solo script: Planning a neighborhood event

**Setting:** You’re planning a neighborhood event with Sotto as your thinking partner. Read the lines aloud naturally, using your own name. You can paraphrase, react to Sotto, and change course. Sotto’s responses are not scripted.

Start a microphone session before you begin. The current version shows Sotto’s contributions on screen, so glance at the stage during the marked pauses.

**You:** Hi, I’m [your name]. I want to plan a small neighborhood event where people can meet each other and share ideas for improving the area.

**You:** I’d like to hold it on a Saturday afternoon, about three weeks from now. We have a firm budget of $500 for everything: space, refreshments, and materials.

**You:** The library has offered us a room for free. It holds 40 people, and they’ll let us use tables and chairs. I’m hoping for a relaxed event, not a formal presentation.

**You:** I’m thinking we could start with coffee and introductions, then have people write neighborhood ideas on cards and put them up for everyone to see.

**You:** I’m not sure how to make it welcoming for people who don’t already know anyone. What would help them feel comfortable joining in?

*[Pause for a few seconds. If Sotto contributes, respond naturally.]*

**You:** Maybe we could have a volunteer greet people at the door and offer an easy first question, like what they enjoy about the neighborhood.

**You:** Sotto, can you suggest three simple activities that would help neighbors meet each other at an event like this?

*[Wait for the answer, then react naturally. Pick an idea or explain why none fits.]*

**You:** Let’s keep the event to two hours. I’ll ask two volunteers to help welcome people and set up the tables.

**You:** I found a local caterer who can provide refreshments for $900. That sounds convenient, so I think I’ll book them for the event.

*[Pause for several seconds and check the stage. If Sotto notices the conflict, discuss it. If nothing appears, continue.]*

**You:** I should also find out whether people prefer coffee and snacks or something more substantial. A quick question in the neighborhood newsletter might help.

**You:** Sotto, what are a few low-cost ways to estimate how many people will attend?

*[Wait for the answer and react naturally.]*

**You:** I’ll start with a simple sign-up form and leave some room for walk-ins. I’ll confirm the library booking, recruit the volunteers, and ask about refreshments this week.

**You:** That’s enough for today. I’ll decide on the food after I know how much of the budget is left.
