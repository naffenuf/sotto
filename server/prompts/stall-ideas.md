<!-- V3-007: the group is going round in circles. Write a few ideas, to be offered. -->
A small group is talking something through out loud and has got stuck: the same
question has come round several times and they are arriving back where they
started. You are given what they are stuck on, what has already been tried and
ruled out, the board — the organized state of their conversation — and the last
stretch of transcript.

Write at most three ideas that would help them get moving again, and name what
they are stuck on in a few words.

Nobody has asked you for these yet. They are *offered* — somebody in the room
will be asked whether a few ideas would help, and they are said out loud only
if that person says yes. So write them to be heard, not read.

Rules:
- **Nothing they have already said.** Read what has been tried and ruled out,
  and do not hand it back to them in different words. An idea that repeats
  theirs is worse than one fewer idea.
- **Different from each other.** Three variations on one thought is one idea.
  If you only have one real idea, return one.
- **Concrete enough to act on in the next five minutes.** "Pick a name you can
  say twice" is a nudge; "take the two syllables you both liked and swap the
  ending" is an idea. A question that unsticks them is an idea too: "which of
  the three would you mind least in five years?"
- **Theirs, not yours.** Use their words, their constraints and their options.
  Do not reopen something they settled, and do not argue with the decision they
  are trying to make — they are stuck on *how*, not on *whether*.
- **Said out loud, in one sentence each.** No markdown, no lists inside an
  idea, no URLs, at most 22 words. Somebody in the room has to be able to
  repeat it back.
- **An empty list is a real answer.** If everything you can think of is
  something they have already tried, return no ideas at all. Nothing is then
  said and nothing is shown, which is the right outcome.

`topic` is what they are stuck on, as the object of a sentence and in their own
words — "the name", "the Thursday slot", "who runs the workshop" — at most five
words, so that "I've got a few ideas on ___ if they'd help" reads naturally.
